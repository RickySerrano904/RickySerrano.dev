This was my first custom PC build and the project that sparked my interest in computers and IT. 
Before this build, I had virtually no experience with computers beyond playing games on laptops and 
using basic software. Since then, I've come a long way, earning a degree in IT and going on to work 
professionally in the field. Looking back on this project now, it's rewarding to see just how far 
I've come since I first started.

I initially decided to build my own PC after a conversation with a friend. He told me that building a 
computer from scratch would give you much greater bang-for-your-buck compared to a prebuilt system, 
but that the biggest reward would be the satisfaction of seeing your little creation glowing 
in the corner of your room every day.

He was absolutely right. Seeing the finished product and knowing that I had put it together myself was 
incredibly satisfying. In many ways, it gave me the same sense of accomplishment that comes from restoring 
a classic car—taking the time to learn how everything fits together, adding personal touches that reflect 
your personality, and eventually knowing the machine inside and out.

The best part is being able to enjoy the result of all that effort. With a classic car, that means finally 
taking it out for a cruise. In my case, it meant firing up a heavily modded Fallout 4 and watching the PC 
I built handle it with ease.


I started my planning by envisioning where the PC would sit in my room. I wanted to place it on the left side of my desk, 
which meant that a conventional case layout would leave the glass panel facing away from me. While this was not a major 
issue, I wanted all the fancy components inside the PC to be on full display for me.

Because of this, I began looking for a case with a reverse layout and eventually found the darkFlash V22. 
This mid-tower case uses an unconventional design that rotates the motherboard 90 degrees, placing the rear I/O at 
the top of the case instead of the back. It also positions the GPU vertically, which makes for a very unique look. 
Most importantly, its reversed layout allowed me to display the internal components on the side facing me, making it a 
perfect fit for what I had envisioned.

I used 'pcpartpicker.com' to research compatible components and create a preliminary parts list. 
I had a rough idea of what I wanted: well-balanced components that would not break 
the bank while still letting me play games with my friends at solid graphics settings. This build dates back 
to 2020, so some of the components look dated beside current hardware. Despite this, it has served me extremely 
well over the years and taught me a great deal about the PC-building process.

Since completing this build, I've upgraded the GPU to an NVIDIA RTX 4080, which provided a significant improvement in 
gaming performance. I also expanded the storage capacity by adding a couple more SSDs. For now, I plan to continue using 
this PC as my primary system, though I'm already looking forward to starting a new custom build in the near future.
