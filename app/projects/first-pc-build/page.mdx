This was my first custom PC build and the project that got me comfortable working directly with computer hardware. Choosing each component, checking compatibility, and assembling the system piece by piece made the process far more personal than buying a prebuilt machine.

I started with PCPartPicker to research compatible components and create a preliminary parts list. I had a rough idea of what I wanted: a mid-tower case and well-balanced components that would not break the bank while still letting me play games with my friends at solid graphics settings. This build dates back to 2020, so some of the components look dated beside current hardware. At the time, though, it served me well and taught me a great deal about the PC-building process.

A secondary goal was to find a mid-tower case with its glass panel on the opposite side. I planned to place the PC in a corner, where a conventional layout would have left the panel facing away from me. I also needed good airflow and room for a liquid CPU cooler. The darkFlash V22 met those requirements with its reverse layout: the I/O is positioned at the top rather than the back, allowing the glass panel to face the opposite direction. Its required vertical GPU mount also gave the build a distinctive visual element.

## Build Goals

- Build a balanced PC capable of 60 FPS gaming at 1440p.
- Use a mid-tower case with good airflow and a layout that places the glass panel on the opposite side of most cases.
- Learn the complete assembly process, from component selection through cable routing and first boot.
- Choose reliable parts that work well together instead of optimizing around a single specification.
- Create a strong foundation for future upgrades and later custom PC builds.

## What I Learned

This build taught me that a successful PC is more than a collection of impressive parts. Planning the order of assembly, understanding where cables need to go, and giving every component enough physical space all matter just as much as the specifications on the box.

It also gave me the confidence to take on the PC projects that followed. Every later build has benefited from the fundamentals I learned here: be deliberate with parts, take your time during assembly, and keep the finished system practical as well as visually considered.
